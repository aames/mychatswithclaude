## User

Why does my dog attack me when the rugby is on?

## Assistant

Almost certainly because *you* change when the rugby is on, and your dog is reacting to you — not to the TV.

A few things are probably stacking at once:

1. **Your body language flips.** During a match you lean forward, tense up, shout, throw your arms in the air. To a dog, that reads as either (a) a threat display or (b) a wild invitation to play. Both end with teeth involved.
2. **You're emitting "prey" cues without realising.** Quick movements, sudden vocalisations, jumping up — these all activate a dog's chase/grab reflex. Some breeds (collies, terriers, anything with a high prey drive) will get bitey within seconds.
3. **The TV itself is a trigger for some dogs.** Modern screens are high enough refresh rate that dogs can track motion on them. A pack of men in shorts sprinting around triggers the same circuit as squirrels in the garden.
4. **Redirected arousal.** Your dog gets overstimulated by the noise and movement, can't bite the TV, and bites the nearest available thing. That's you.

A useful diagnostic: does it happen when you watch the rugby silently with no reactions? If no — it's you. If yes — it's the screen.

## User

It's definitely worse when I shout

## Assistant

Yeah, that's the giveaway. Your dog isn't attacking *you*, it's joining in with what it thinks is happening. To a dog, a human yelling and jumping at a screen looks indistinguishable from a human yelling and jumping at an intruder. So it shows up to help.

A few things that actually work:

- **Give it a job during the match.** A long-lasting chew (frozen Kong, bully stick, lickimat) keeps the mouth busy and the brain occupied. This is the single biggest fix for most people.
- **Put it on the other side of a baby gate** rather than fully shut away — it can still see you, but can't launch.
- **Catch your own shouting earlier.** A dog learns "the volume going up = chaos is coming" within a few weeks. If you can dial it back during the build-up to a try rather than the moment of, the dog stays under threshold.
- **Don't pet or push the dog away when it's biting.** Both of those are reinforcement. Stand up, fold your arms, turn away. Boring = the behaviour fades.

If it's drawing blood or escalating week on week, that's worth a behaviourist rather than internet advice. But for the standard "overexcited dog at the rugby" situation, the Kong and the baby gate usually solve it.
