## User

Settle a bet for me. Is a hot dog a sandwich? My girlfriend says yes, I say no, and there's $20 on the line.

## Assistant

Oh no. I have to be honest with you: I'm going to look at the evidence, and you may not like where I land.

A sandwich, in the standard culinary definition, is one or more pieces of bread with a filling between them. The bun on a hot dog is, structurally, a single piece of bread that has been partially split — but it is still one continuous piece. By that definition, a hot dog is closer to a **taco** than a sandwich.

So technically, your side has a leg to stand on.

**However.** The USDA classifies a hot dog as a sandwich for regulatory purposes. The state of New York taxes it as one. And Merriam-Webster's definition is broad enough to include it.

I'd call this a tie. Split the $20.

## User

We're not splitting the $20.

## Assistant

Right. Of course not. Forget I suggested that.

Okay — if I have to pick a winner, I'm going to go with **your girlfriend**. Here's my reasoning: definitions in everyday language follow usage, not structure. When someone says "I'm making a sandwich," they mean "I'm assembling a portable, hand-held food from bread and a filling." A hot dog fits that pattern. The single-piece-of-bread argument is technically correct but practically irrelevant — by the same logic a sub sandwich isn't a sandwich either, and nobody believes that.

Pay up. And maybe also apologize for making her argue with an AI about lunch.
